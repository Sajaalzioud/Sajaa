# TRIAD: Frequently Asked Questions for Investors

---

## **About TRIAD & The Model**

### Q: Why is the integrated multi-department model better than three separate clinics?

**A:** Three key advantages:

1. **Patient convenience**: Families book once, get coordinated assessment across all specialties, single billing. This improves patient experience and retention.
2. **Operational efficiency**: Shared reception, IT infrastructure, admin, marketing. Shared services (hydrotherapy, wellness) leverage capacity across departments.
3. **Revenue synergies**: Cross-referrals between departments improve utilization. A parent with a stroke (neurology) often needs concurrent orthopedic recovery. A child with cerebral palsy benefits from both pediatric and orthopedic therapy.

Three separate clinics compete for the same patients; TRIAD captures them all.

---

### Q: Why would patients choose TRIAD over established solo practitioners?

**A:** TRIAD offers five things solo clinics don't:

1. **Integrated care**: One appointment, all three specialties available.
2. **Professional environment**: Modern facility, IT systems, insurance panel integration.
3. **Founder expertise**: Each founder is a recognized specialist with 6+ years proven track record.
4. **Convenience**: Parking, reception, payment — all in one place.
5. **Trust**: Insurance company and corporate partnerships (employee wellness programs) often prefer established, multi-specialty centers.

For high-income New Cairo families, this is a premium positioning.

---

### Q: Can the model scale to other locations?

**A:** Yes, absolutely. This is a **unit-economics model**: one location validates the business, then replication is capital-efficient.

By Year 3, a second location in similar demographics (e.g., Sheikh Zayed, Maadi) would:
- Share marketing, HR, IT management (head office)
- Use proven staffing/equipment templates
- Reduce launch costs by 20–30% (vendor bulk discounts, known operations)
- Break even faster (reputation pre-built)

Target: 3–5 locations by Year 7, positioning TRIAD as Egypt's first multi-location rehabilitation group.

## **Financial Viability**

### Q: Why is therapy a good recurring-revenue business?

**A:** Therapy is among the stickiest B2C health services:

1. **Duration**: Patients attend 2–3x/week for 6–12 weeks (minimum), often longer.
2. **Outcomes**: Progress is visible; patients see improvement and continue.
3. **Referrals**: Therapist-patient relationships drive word-of-mouth and high referral rates.
4. **Insurance**: Private insurance covers ongoing therapy; reduces price sensitivity.

Churn is typically 5–15% monthly once patient finds "their therapist." Contrast with a dermatology clinic (one-off visits) or surgery (one procedure).

---

### Q: What are the key revenue drivers?

**A:** Three:

1. **Volume**: Clinical sessions (therapist-patient pairs). Each therapist does 6–8 sessions/day, 5 days/week. At 250 EGP/session (ortho), 300 EGP (neuro), 200 EGP (pediatric), a single therapist generates ~6M EGP annually.

2. **Payer mix**: 40% out-of-pocket, 40% private insurance, 20% corporate wellness/employee programs. Insurance & corporate clients are higher-margin and lower-churn.

3. **Shared services**: Hydrotherapy, wellness classes, home visits, corporate programs generate incremental revenue without significant additional therapist hiring.

## **Risk & Mitigation**

### Q: What if patient acquisition is slower than projected?

**A:** Mitigation:

1. **Pre-launch partnerships**: Secure referral agreements with 10–15 local doctors/hospitals *before* opening.
2. **Corporate contracts**: Lock in 2–3 corporate wellness contracts pre-launch (guarantees minimum monthly sessions).
3. **Marketing efficiency**: Digital marketing (Google, Instagram) and affiliate doctor networks are cost-effective in New Cairo.
4. **Flexible staffing**: Start with 2–3 core therapists per department; hire additional staff only as utilization rises above 60%.

Conservative model assumes 50% utilization by Month 6. Even if delayed to Month 9, breakeven shifts from Month 12 to Month 15—still within funding runway.

---

### Q: What if founder/key therapist leaves?

**A:** Each department is led by a founder (Nagham, Saja Khader, Saja Ali), but operations don't depend on them alone:

1. **Recruiting**: New Cairo has a growing pool of trained physiotherapists and occupational therapists. Salary benchmark is 3.5–5K EGP/month for experienced staff.
2. **Standardization**: SOPs documented; patients see department (not individual) as the brand.
3. **Founder commitment**: Founders are equity holders with long-term vision; turnover incentives align their interests with investor returns.

If a founder departs, replacement hiring adds 1–2 months to service continuity but doesn't threaten the business model.

---

### Q: What's the competitive risk?

**A:** New Cairo's rehab market is fragmented (100+ solo practitioners, ~10 small clinics). Competitive moat for TRIAD:

1. **Integrated model**: No existing competitor combines all three specialties under one roof.
2. **Founder brand**: Nagham, Saja Khader, Saja Ali have reputation; patients follow them.
3. **Professional environment**: Premium facility, branding, insurance integration—solo practitioners can't match.
4. **Scale**: By Year 3, TRIAD's size and patient volume will make it the destination for rehabilitation in New Cairo.

Risk: A large hospital network launches a competing rehab center. Mitigation: First-mover advantage, patient loyalty, and TRIAD's integrated model is harder to replicate than opening a generic clinic.

---

### Q: What are the regulatory risks?

**A:** Egypt's Ministry of Health regulates private clinics. Requirements:

1. **Licensing**: Medical director license (founder holds), clinic license, and per-therapist registration.
2. **Insurance panels**: Registration with major insurers (Axa, Cigna, CIHC, Medinet) takes 2–3 months but is routine.
3. **Compliance**: Infection control, record-keeping, staff qualifications—all standard for modern clinics. SOPs document compliance.

**Mitigation**: TRIAD has retained local healthcare legal counsel to manage licensing and insurance panel applications during setup.

## **Investment Mechanics**

### Q: What are the investment terms?

**A:** Open to negotiation; typical structure:

- **Investment Type**: Equity stake (percentage ownership) or convertible note (if preferred).
- **Timeline**: Funds deployed over Months 1–3 (facility setup, equipment, payroll).
- **Governance**: Board seat, quarterly reporting, veto rights on major decisions (expansion, sale, additional fundraising).
- **Dividend Policy**: 30–50% of annual profits distributed annually (if profitable).
- **Liquidation**: In case of sale, investors receive proportional share of proceeds after liabilities.

### Q: Are there tax implications for investors?

**A:** Egypt's tax on capital gains and dividends:

- **Capital gains**: 10% on profits when equity is sold (deferred).
- **Dividends**: 10% corporate tax (TRIAD pays), then personal income tax on investor's portion (marginal rate varies).

**Mitigation**: Consult with your tax advisor on structuring (holding company, investment vehicle, etc.). TRIAD's accountant can provide tax projections during due diligence.

### Q: What happens if I have concerns after due diligence?

**A:** Investors retain the right to:

- **Request clarifications**: Any financial, operational, or clinical question.
- **Renegotiate terms**: Equity %, dividend policy, governance rights.
- **Walk away**: No obligation to proceed; can exit process without penalty.

Transparency and founder flexibility are critical to building investor confidence.
